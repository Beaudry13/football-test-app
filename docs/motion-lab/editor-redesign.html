Motion Lab Editor Redesign

The engine is finished and proven. This sprint reorganizes the controls that operate it so a coach's mental model, move players → draw assignments → set the ball → press play, is what the screen shows, without turning the editor into a wizard or shrinking the field.

Everything below was worked in passes: audit of the live editor at 1366×768, a control priority map, three genuinely different layouts, a chosen direction taken through three revisions with a critique between each, then scenario walk-throughs and a laptop check. The full written handoff (22 sections, P0/P1/P2 list) is the companion document; this page is the visual record.

1 · What the current editor looks like, and where it fights the coach

Measured on the live prototype at 1366×768: three chrome bars (55 + 50 + 54 px) leave the field 585 px tall, 76% of the screen. Twenty-five buttons are visible before anything is selected. The structure is sound. The problems are hierarchy, mode, wording and noise.

Current editor, player Z selected. Numbered callouts are explained below.
1Move / Draw is a global mode switch. The most important interaction in the product (draw his assignment) is behind a mode toggle that also changes what dragging a player means. This is the biggest source of "what do I click now".
2Two hints, both weak. A muted, truncated instruction in the top bar and another in the context row. Neither is where the eye goes.
3Present and Play compete. Two gold primaries on screen at once; Present is teaching, not authoring, yet it is the loudest control.
4The ball lives at the top right, far from Play and reading like a settings menu. "🏈 Ball: Play Action → RB → Z · then …" is engine syntax.
5View switch looks like an editing mode. Overhead / Coach / Player sits between Undo and Move, and switching silently disables editing.
6Selected-player controls arrive at equal weight. Timing, Speed and End (Auto/Continue/Settle) are three segmented controls with nine buttons, plus Assignment and Engage. Continue/Settle is engine vocabulary.
7"Clear All Paths" is always on screen, right-aligned and destructive, for something used once a play.
8Playback bar mixes display preferences (Paths filter, Labels) with transport, and has no step control.
9Setup items (Players, Look, Situation) share a row with the hint, so the resting state reads as three unrelated menus.
10Keyboard badges on every button (V, D, B, Space) add texture without teaching much; most coaches will use the mouse.

What is right and must stay

  • The field is the input: ball targets, catch points, meet points and viewers are picked by clicking on the field, with dashed rings on the legal choices. Keep every one of those flows.
  • Fixed-height bars, so the field never jumps when the selection changes.
  • Football-friendly words already in use: Pre-Snap / On Snap / Delayed, Controlled / Normal / Fast, "QB holds 0.6 s at the top of his drop for Y's timing".
  • Derived orientation. Body and look are computed, shown on the selected player, and never asked of the coach.

2 · Control priority map

Classified by how often a coach touches each control while building an ordinary play, not by how important the engine thinks it is. Nothing is removed; the levels decide where a control lives and how loud it is.

Primary · always one click away

  • Select and move a player (drag, no mode)
  • Draw his assignment
  • Ball action
  • Play / Pause, Restart, scrub
  • Undo / Redo
  • Play name and saved state

Secondary · visible in context

  • Timing: Pre-snap / On snap / Delayed
  • Blocks… (engage) and Releases
  • Adjust route (anchors) / Redraw
  • Overhead / Coach / Player view
  • Present
  • Situation chip
  • Formation: saved looks, add / remove player
  • Playback rate, Display (paths filter, labels, markings)

Advanced · under More or Advanced

  • Speed tier per player
  • After the route: keep running / stop (continue / settle)
  • Delay seconds
  • Copy to… / Mirror to…
  • Throw point (Throw From Here)
  • Then… second ball action
  • Play-action fake target
  • Hash shift, markings on/off
  • Rename / Remove player, Clear all routes

3 · Three layout directions

Three different answers to "where do controls live", each evaluated against field space at 1366×768 (the field is height-bound there: every horizontal bar costs field height directly), speed, learnability, clutter, discoverability, advanced access, laptop and meeting-room use.

A · Floating controls near the player

One thin top bar, no strip. Selecting a player pops a small card beside him with Draw / Timing / More. The ball and transport share a bottom dock.

Field spaceBest (no strip)
SpeedControls at the cursor
LearnabilityCard jumps around
ClutterCard covers the play it edits
Advanced accessNested popovers
Meeting roomHard to project and point at

B · Tool rail + inspector

A vertical rail (Select, Draw, Ball, Block, Present) on the left, a persistent inspector on the right showing the selected player's every property.

Field space−9% height at 1366, worse at 1440×900
SpeedEverything visible, more travel
LearnabilityFamiliar design-tool pattern
ClutterPermanent panel, dashboard feel
Advanced accessAlways exposed
Meeting roomField shrinks on projection

C · Field-first: one strip above, one dock below

Top bar = the play file. A single fixed strip above the field = whoever is selected (or the setup when nobody is). The dock = the ball and the transport, in that order.

Field space+20 px over today; +64 px in Present
SpeedStrip is one glance above the field
LearnabilityThree stable zones
ClutterStrip holds ≤ 7 controls
Advanced accessOne click under More
Meeting roomNothing covers the field
Chosen: C. A loses on the very thing that matters most, the field it covers, and a card that follows the selection cannot be pointed at from across a room. B is the most discoverable and the most "software": it spends field width permanently to show properties a coach changes once a game. C keeps the current bones (which the coach who built a real play already knows), fixes the hierarchy, and still fits three stable zones a first-timer can name. The rest of the sprint refines C.

4 · Direction C, revision 1

First pass: move the ball next to Play, drop the Move/Draw switch, turn the selected-player row into a hierarchy, push setup under one Formation menu.

Revision 1, Z selected. Draw is the one gold button in the strip. Ball sits left of Play in the dock.

Critique of revision 1

  • Still twelve controls in the strip. Timing and Speed as full segments plus Draw, Adjust, Blocks, More is the old row with better colors. Speed is set by position and rarely changed; it moves to More.
  • "Draw assignment" is gold even when the player already has one. After drawing, the primary action is no longer drawing; the button should become quiet ("Redraw") and the strip should show what he does instead.
  • Nothing tells the coach how to draw without pressing a button. A first-timer sees "Draw assignment", clicks it, then has to know to drag on the field. The field itself should offer the drawing affordance on the selected player.
  • The ball button reads "Ball ▾" when empty. For a play with no ball action, the third step should be visibly unfinished.
  • The Formation menu buries Situation. Down and distance are read aloud in every meeting; it deserves its own chip.
  • View segment still sits in the top bar with no verb. A coach reads "Player" as a noun.

5 · Revision 2

Revision 2, Z selected with no assignment yet. The route handle on Z (gold grip, pulsing) is new: drag it to draw. Strip is down to seven controls. Ball reads "Set the ball" until it is set.

Critique of revision 2

  • The handle risks being mistaken for a drag target that moves the player. Fix: the handle sits outside the marker, is visibly a different shape (a short gold stub with an arrowhead) and the cursor changes to crosshair over it. Drag on the marker itself always moves. Test on first-time users; keep the Draw button and D as the second path.
  • Timing segment is the only three-way control left, and it earns it. Pre-snap motion is visual and common. Keep it.
  • "Blocks…" is wrong for a defender. The strip verb should follow the side: "Blocks…" for offense, "Engages…" for defense. Same engine flow.
  • Present still gold. Make it an outlined gold button: important, not the primary. Play is the only solid gold button on the screen outside a drawing or ball banner.
  • Step buttons in the dock. Coaches ask "go back a tick" constantly in a room. Add ◁ ▷ (0.1 s) beside the scrub; arrow keys do the same.
  • The situation chip belongs to the field, not the strip. Moved to the dock's right side next to the display toggles, where it stays visible in Present.

6 · Final design

Three stable zones. Top bar is the play: name, saved state, undo, how you are watching, Present. Strip is whoever is selected, or the formation when nobody is. Dock is the play in motion: the ball, then the transport, then how the field is displayed. The field between them is never covered by chrome.

Resting. Nobody selected. One quiet sentence says what to do. "Set the ball" is outlined gold because the play has no ball action yet.
Z selected, no assignment. The strip names him and offers one gold action. His route handle pulses on the field. Timing is visible; everything else is under More.
Drawing. The strip becomes a gold-edged banner; the cursor is a crosshair; the route is a dashed gold line while the mouse is down. Release finishes it. No sticky mode: after the drag the strip returns to the selected state.
Z selected, assignment drawn. The strip now reads what he does: "Route · 9 yds · out". Draw has gone quiet ("Redraw"), Adjust appears, More holds the rest.
More menu for a selected player. Speed, what he does after the route, copy and mirror, clear, rename, remove. Football words; the engine's "continue / settle" appears only as the explanation.
Ball menu. One question, five football answers. Once an action exists the menu shows it in a sentence, with Change, Then…, and an Advanced disclosure for the throw point.
Picking on the field. Same banner treatment as drawing, so "the editor is waiting for a click" always looks the same. Legal targets get the dashed ring; everyone else dims.
Playing. Play becomes Pause, the scrub fills gold, the SNAP tick is blue, time is relative to the snap. Nothing in the strip changes, so the coach can pause and keep editing.
Present. The strip is gone, the field gains 44 px, the top bar keeps only the play name, views, telestration and Exit. The dock keeps transport, situation and display toggles.

Final simplification pass (what the third look removed)

  • Paths filter + Labels → one Display ▾ menu in the dock (Paths: All / Offense / Defense / None; Labels; Field markings). Two persistent controls become one, and the markings toggle leaves the situation popover where it never belonged.
  • Rate segment → a single 1× ▾ pill. Three buttons for something changed once a session.
  • Time readout shows only the current time relative to the snap ("+1.3 s"). The play length was noise; the scrub bar already shows it.
  • Keyboard badges gone from every button; the key appears in the tooltip. The dock lost eight labels and reads as three groups: ball, transport, display.
  • Resting strip is one button and one sentence. Players, Look and Clear All Paths are inside Formation ▾ and the play menu.

Persistent control count at rest: 25 today → 14 final (top bar 8, strip 1, dock 5), with nothing removed from the product.

9 · Renamed, promoted, demoted

Renamed

  • Draw Path → Draw assignment (Redraw once one exists)
  • Edit Path → Adjust
  • Engage… → Blocks… (offense) / Engages… (defense)
  • Release → Releases after the block
  • End: Auto / Continue / Settle → After the route: Keeps running / Stops
  • Clear Path → Clear assignment; Clear All Paths → Clear all assignments
  • Throw From Here → Throw point: set on field
  • Look → Formation (Saved formations)
  • Players ▾ → inside Formation ▾ as Add player
  • Ball: Pass → Z → Pass to Z; empty state Set the ball
  • Then… → Then… (kept), shown as "then pitch to H"
  • Playback "Speed" → Rate (avoids the player Speed tier)

More prominent

  • Draw assignment: the one gold button in the strip
  • The ball: beside Play, outlined gold until set
  • Play: largest control on screen, the only solid gold outside banners
  • The route handle on the selected player
  • Mode banners for drawing and picking
  • What the selected player does, in words, in the strip

Less prominent

  • Move / Draw switch: removed as a persistent control
  • Present: outlined, not solid
  • Speed tier, After the route, Copy, Mirror: under More
  • Clear all assignments: in the play menu
  • Keyboard badges: tooltips only
  • Top-bar hint: removed; one hint line lives in the strip
  • Paths filter, Labels, field markings: one Display ▾ menu in the dock

Intentionally not changed

  • All field-pick flows (receiver, catch point, carrier, partner, meet point, throw point, viewer). They are the best part of the editor.
  • Fixed-height bars and the rule that the field never moves under the cursor.
  • Timing and speed vocabulary. Derived orientation with no coach-facing control. The catch-adjusted toast. Engagement and ball warnings.
  • The engine, path math, ball logic, engagement logic, persistence and the play/look schema. This is a reorganization of controls, not a rebuild.
